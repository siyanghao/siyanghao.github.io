<strong>Siyang Hao</strong> is a Research Associate at the <strong>University of Virginia</strong>, working within the ONR <strong>Multidisciplinary University Research Initiative (MURI)</strong> program. His current work examines how phase differences between swimmers shape the hydrodynamic benefits of robotic fish schooling, supported by a closed-loop water tunnel facility he engineered around an automated, cyber-physical experimental workflow.

He received his Ph.D. in Engineering (Fluid Mechanics) from <strong>Brown University</strong> in 2025, advised by Prof. Kenny Breuer, with a thesis titled "<strong>Surfing on Vortices: Interactions of Flapping Flight in Vortex Wake</strong>". The work combined wind tunnel experiments on live birds, bio-inspired robotic platforms, and the flapping-wing robot <em>Flapperoo</em> &mdash; featured by <em>National Geographic</em> &mdash; to understand how flapping flyers interact with structured vortex wakes.

Siyang earned his M.Sc. in Aerospace and Mechanical Engineering from the <strong>University of Southern California</strong> (USC) in 2019, where he led the compliant flapping-wing ornithopter <a href="https://youtu.be/z90MvEVCOD4">USC Raven</a>, and his B.Sc. in Aerospace Engineering from <strong>Beihang University</strong> (BUAA) in 2017, where he developed the <a href="https://youtu.be/itfVvW8pdqs">Distributed Multi-Vectored Propulsion (DMVP)</a> system.

Research Summary
======
His research interests span <strong>fluid mechanics &amp; bio-inspired robotics</strong>, covering

  * experimental fluid mechanics (PIV, automated wind and water tunnel testing)
  * unsteady aerodynamics, vortex-wake and fluid&ndash;structure interaction
  * animal locomotion, flight mechanics, and robotic flyers and swimmers
  * flow control for wind energy and unmanned vehicles
