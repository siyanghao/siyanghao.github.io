excerpt: 'Accelerometer and metabolic data from European Starlings show that, in solo flight, continuous low-effort flapping reduced energy costs compared with bursts of high-effort flapping, while low-cost birds in small groups used slow, organized burst-coast rhythms. For followers, the organization of the leader''s flapping was more closely associated with the follower''s energy cost than any metric recorded from the follower itself.'
