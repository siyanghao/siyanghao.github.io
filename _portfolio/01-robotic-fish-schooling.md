At the University of Virginia, as part of the ONR **Multidisciplinary University Research Initiative (MURI)** program, this project asks when swimming together actually pays off. Using pairs and small groups of robotic swimmers, we measure how the **phase difference** between neighbouring flappers &mdash; and the geometry of the formation &mdash; changes the thrust, power and efficiency of each individual.

The experiments run in a **closed-loop water tunnel facility** built around an automated, cyber-physical workflow: motion control, force sensing and flow measurement are synchronized and script-driven, so large parameter sweeps can be collected and processed without manual intervention.

Related work on formation geometry and group swimming efficiency was carried out as a Visiting Scientist at the University of California, Riverside.
